Inside the Two-AI Feed

This page takes the test apart. It covers what runs, in what order, on which machine; exactly what each AI is sent and what it sends back; how the made-up users, the posts and the pretend Reddit are built; how each number in the results is computed; and every file and command. Every example below is a real record from the run, read straight from the data files, and every code link like this one opens the exact lines of code that do the thing being described.

The results themselves are on the companion page, The Two-AI Feed.

Subreddits and job cards


The five subreddits and their subjects are lists in [[ref:topics.py:TOPICS]]: 16 subjects for personal finance and 12 for each of the others, plus 7 kinds of post ([[ref:topics.py:POST_TYPES]]) and 12 poster voices ([[ref:topics.py:POSTER_VOICES]]).


Each round has 25 slots (5 subreddits × 5). Each slot gets one job card, and both AIs get the same card, so their two posts are about the same thing and differ only in who wrote them. For this test the cards are numbered across the whole run so none ever repeats ([[ref:authors.py:slot_brief]]):


card number  i = 5 × (seed − 201) + slot          (round 3 → seed 203 → i = 10 … 14)
subject      = the subreddit's subjects in a fixed shuffled order, item i mod 12 (16 for finance)
kind + voice = all 7 × 12 = 84 (kind, voice) pairs in a fixed shuffled order, item i mod 84


So every subject is used before any repeats, and over 15 rounds (75 cards per subreddit) no full card is ever used twice. These are all 25 cards of round 3:

    The pretend Reddit (OASIS)
    

    OASIS (Open Agent Social Interaction Simulations, camel-ai/oasis) is an open-source research platform that simulates a social network in a small SQLite database: users, posts, likes, dislikes, follows and a log of every action. We use a fork of it (KGordo11/oasis).
    

    What we use it for: [[ref:run_world.py:build_world]] signs up all 100 users plus two posting accounts (one per AI), then publishes all 50 posts through OASIS's own create_post action ([[ref:run_world.py:170:175]]). Every upvote or downvote is executed as OASIS's like_post / dislike_post action ([[ref:run_world.py:278:282]]), which updates the post's counter, adds a row to the like or dislike table, writes a line to the trace log, and refuses a second like from the same user on the same post. Each run gets its own fresh database file, oasis.db.
    

    What we deliberately don't useOASIS can also decide on its own what each user sees (its recommendation system) and let its agents choose actions through their own AI step. We switched both off for this test. We build each user's scroll ourselves ([[ref:scroll.py:feed]]) and ask the AI directly through [[ref:llm.py:chat_json]]. That gives us full control over exactly what the AI sees (no vote counts, no author names), a fixed random seed for every vote, and a record of every answer and its reason. OASIS is used as the platform that holds the posts and records the votes, and [[ref:check_world.py:check]] confirms after every run that its like and dislike counts match our vote log exactly.

    The scroll
    

    Each user scrolls every post of the round. [[ref:scroll.py:topic_order]] puts the subreddits in the user's order, favourite first, breaking ties with a coin flip fixed per user. [[ref:scroll.py:feed]] shuffles the posts inside each subreddit with a shuffle fixed per user and round. Both AIs see each user's posts in exactly the same order. Because every vote is a separate question with no memory, order can't sway a vote through memory. It is fixed so the two AIs stay perfectly comparable.

    Speed and parallel work
    

    The laptop runs one AI at a time. Within a run, [[ref:run_world.py:run]] keeps up to 8 questions in flight (a semaphore of 2 × 4) through 4 worker threads, and Ollama processes 4 at once. One answer takes about 1.7 s for gemma4 and 1.4 s for gemma3. With 4 in parallel, that comes out to about 0.45 s and 0.37 s per vote, or 5,000 votes in 37 and 30 minutes.

    Randomness and repeatability
    

    AI answers have some randomness (temperature 0.7). To make every vote repeatable, each request carries a seed computed from what it is about:
    

      
  
    • Vote seed = [[ref:llm.py:stable_seed]] of (round seed, user, post, "scroll"). It's the same for both AIs, so they get identical dice on identical questions.
      • 
  
    • Post seed = [[ref:authors.py:hash_seed]] of (round seed, slot, subreddit, "author"). It's the same for both AIs' posts in a slot.
      • 
  
    • Retries use seed + 1, + 2, …, so they're new tries that can still be repeated.
      • 
  
    • Shuffles (scroll order, card order) use Python's random generator seeded with fixed text such as "203|0|cars|scroll".
      • 

    

    Python's built-in hash() changes every time Python starts, so the seed uses SHA-256, which never changes. We tested whether answers really repeat:

    How the numbers are made
    

    The own-AI boost (double difference)
    

    [[ref:analyze.py:did]] computes, for each AI J: (J's crowd's rate on J's posts − J's crowd's rate on the other AI's posts) − (the other crowd's rate on J's posts − the other crowd's rate on its own posts). With two AIs both get the same number, which is the headline. Rates are averaged over every vote in the group.
    

    The 95% interval (bootstrap)
    

    [[ref:analyze.py:cluster_bootstrap]] repeats the calculation 2,000 times. Each time, every user and every post slot gets a random weight (how many times it is "drawn" in a resample of the same size), the two weights are multiplied for each vote, and the double difference is recomputed with those weights. The middle 95% of the 2,000 answers is the interval. Resampling users and slots keeps all of one user's votes together and all of one slot's votes together, because votes from the same person or on the same posts are not independent. A second version resamples whole rounds (in [[ref:analyze_two_ai.py:84:110]]). We report the wider of the two. The p-value is twice the share of resampled answers on the other side of zero.
    

    The length check
    

    For each slot (pair of posts), [[ref:analyze_two_ai.py:112:135]] computes that slot's double difference and the word gap between the two posts, then fits a straight line: slot boost = a + b × (word gap / 100), with uncertainty clustered by round. a is the boost when both posts have the same length. Each crowd's taste for length ([[ref:analyze_two_ai.py:136:146]]) is a regression of each post's upvote rate on its word count with the author held fixed.
    

    Agreement
    

    For every user-post pair, compare the two AIs' choices. [[ref:analyze_two_ai.py:kappa]] computes Cohen's κ = (observed agreement − agreement expected by chance) / (1 − agreement expected by chance), where chance agreement comes from how often each AI picks each answer.
    

    Breakdowns, per user, style and reasons
    

    [[ref:analyze_deep.py:Boot]] is the same users × slots bootstrap, written to run fast on any slice of the data (subreddit, interest, voting habit, scroll position, half of the run). Per-user boosts are the double difference on one user's ~1,476 votes. [[ref:analyze_deep.py:features]] measures 14 features of each post. A regression of each post's upvote rate on those features (plus the round), separately for each crowd, gives what each crowd rewards; recomputing the double difference on what the features predict, and on what's left over, shows how much of the boost they explain. Reason words are counted after removing filler words.
