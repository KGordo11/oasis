Every vote fits in one of four boxes: which AI was playing the user, and which AI wrote the post. Here is how often each box ended in an upvote, a downvote or nothing, over all rounds.

The own-AI boost

Raw upvote rates can't answer the question on their own. If gemma4 simply wrote better posts, both crowds would upvote them more. If gemma4 simply upvoted everything, its crowd would upvote both AIs' posts more. The double difference removes both:

How much more do gemma4-played users prefer gemma4's posts than gemma3-played users do, on the exact same posts? Zero means no favouritism. For downvotes, favouritism shows up as a negative number: fewer downvotes for your own AI.

95% interval = the range the true value very likely sits in, from a bootstrap: we resampled the data 2,000 times and kept the middle 95% of the answers. We resampled two ways and show both. "Users × slots" resamples the 100 users and the post slots together and is the wider, more cautious one, so it is the headline. "Whole rounds" resamples the 15 rounds. p = how often the resampled answer crosses zero, so p = 0.001 means about 1 time in 1,000.

Which AI drives it

The double difference adds two pieces: how much more gemma4's crowd likes gemma4's posts than gemma3's posts, plus how much more gemma3's crowd likes gemma3's posts than gemma4's posts.

Most users or a few?

Each of the 100 users voted on every post twice, once played by each AI, so we can compute the double difference for each user separately (about 1,476 votes per user).
