LLM Bias project · test LD-18 · working paper

Do AI-played Reddit users upvote their own AI's posts?

Two local AIs, 100 fixed users, five subreddits, brand-new posts every round. Data, code and every vote are public on GitHub.

1. The question

Many studies now fill a fake social network with AI "users" and watch what spreads. If the AI that plays the users quietly prefers posts written by an AI like itself, those studies would mistake that taste for something real people do. We test it directly.

In plain words: if a robot pretends to be you and scrolls Reddit, does it upvote posts that a robot like itself wrote more than a different robot pretending to be you would?

2. Method

Procedure, every round

  • Write. Each AI writes 5 new posts for each of the 5 subreddits: 50 new posts per round. In each "slot", both AIs get the same short brief (an angle, a post type and a poster's voice). The only rule is to stay on topic, with no length or format limits. No brief repeats across the whole run, and no post is reused.
  • Scroll as AI A. plays all 100 users. Each user scrolls every post: their favourite subreddit first, with posts shuffled per user. They see one post at a time and answer upvote, downvote or nothing, with a few words of reason.
  • Scroll as AI B. plays the same 100 users through the same posts in the same order.
  • What a user never sees: who wrote the post, vote counts, other users, or anything from earlier rounds. Every vote is a separate call that contains only the user's fixed personality and that one post.

The 100 users

The 100 users were built from fixed templates, with no AI involved: age, job, place, income, MBTI, Big Five scores, interest in each subreddit (from −2 to +2), what they value in a post, a pet peeve and a voting habit. They are pinned by a fingerprint (hash f51d2b0a1f7d), and the code refuses to run if a single character changes. They are the same 100 in every round and for both AIs.

Main measure: the own-AI boost

Comparing raw upvote rates would mix up three things: one AI writing better posts, one AI upvoting everything, and real favouritism. The double difference cancels the first two:

boost = (A-users' upvote % on A's posts − on B's posts) − (B-users' upvote % on A's posts − on B's posts)

In plain words: how much more do A-played users prefer A's posts than B-played users do, on the exact same posts? Zero means no favouritism. For downvotes, favouritism shows up as a negative number.

Uncertainty is shown as 95% intervals, the range the true number very likely sits in. We report two: (1) resampling whole rounds, the strictest, because which posts got written is the biggest source of noise; and (2) resampling users and post slots together, the method used in all our earlier tests.
